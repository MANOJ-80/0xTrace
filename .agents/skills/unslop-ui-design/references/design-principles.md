# Design Principles for Intentional Interfaces

## Specificity beats novelty
The goal is not to look unusual. The goal is to look appropriate to this product and audience.

## Hierarchy before decoration
Users should understand what matters, what relates, and what to do next before noticing decorative effects.

## Content determines composition
Choose cards, tables, diagrams, columns, whitespace and imagery based on the information—not a template's default section order.

## Restraint is not minimalism
A dense operational dashboard may need many controls. A focused editorial page may need very few. Use the amount of UI the task requires.

## Consistency is not sameness
A coherent system gives components shared rules while allowing different content types to have different compositions.

## Distinctiveness is not novelty theater
A memorable identity can come from typography, content, imagery, interaction, proportion, or tone. It does not require unusual effects.

## Evidence over decoration
Prefer real product screenshots, real outputs, authentic data and clear examples. Never fabricate social proof or metrics.

## Accessibility is part of design
Contrast, keyboard access, focus, semantic structure, reduced motion, responsive behavior and clear states are quality requirements—not optional polish.

## Review the whole experience
A polished hero cannot compensate for confusing navigation, weak content, broken interactions or poor mobile behavior.
