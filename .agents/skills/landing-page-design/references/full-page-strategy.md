# Full-page strategy

Read this reference for a full landing-page build. For a focused copy, conversion, or section change, use only the relevant guidance from the main skill.

## Intake

Use this as a discovery checklist, not a required interview. First inspect the project brief, product docs or code, existing pages, brand assets, and design tokens. Do not ask the user to repeat information already available. Ask one concise batch of questions only for missing decisions that would materially change the offer, audience, or primary action. Infer low-impact choices from the existing product and design system.

**Purpose**

- What is the one primary action (trial, demo, buy, waitlist, or download)?
- What is the offer, and exactly what does the visitor get?
- What counts as a conversion (click, signup, or purchase)?

**Audience and context**

- Who is the ideal customer profile?
- What problem are they trying to solve?
- What are their main objections?
- What is the traffic source (ads, search, social, or email)?
- What do visitors already know when they land?

**Proof and assets**

- What verified proof exists (logos, testimonials, numbers, or case studies)?
- Are approved screenshots, demo videos, or product GIFs available?
- Are guarantee, refund, or cancellation terms defined?

**Constraints**

- What brand voice and design direction fit?
- Is mobile the primary context?

If no material decision is blocked, continue with reasonable low-impact assumptions and state them briefly. Never invent product claims, customer proof, prices, guarantees, legal terms, or other facts. If factual content is missing, omit it or use clearly marked prototype placeholders; do not stall the build.

## Page structure

Choose only sections that support the offer and available facts.

**Above the fold**

1. Headline: outcome plus audience.
2. Subheadline: explain how and add specificity.
3. Primary CTA: clear verb plus what the visitor gets.
4. Verified proof, when available.
5. Relevant hero visual, when an approved product asset or suitable illustration is available.

**Middle: make the case**

6. Problem-to-solution section.
7. Three to five outcome-driven benefits.
8. How it works, when the workflow is known.
9. Verified social proof, when available.

**Bottom: handle objections**

10. FAQ only when it answers real visitor questions.
11. Risk reversal only when its terms are real and approved.
12. Final CTA consistent with the primary action.

A separate tagline section is optional; do not add one when it repeats the hero or interrupts the page's argument.

## Layout selection

Choose one layout and briefly say why:

| Type | Use when |
|---|---|
| Classic hero plus sections | The product is understandable from a hero screenshot. |
| Long-form story | Visitors need education or reassurance before acting. |
| Minimal conversion page | Traffic has high intent, or the offer is short, such as a download or waitlist. |
| Comparison page | Search intent includes alternatives; pair with SEO guidance when relevant. |

## Build order

Work section by section. A common order is hero, benefits, how it works, proof, FAQ, and final CTA. Include only sections supported by the offer and available facts. Never rebuild the whole page on each iteration; section-by-section work keeps diffs reviewable.

## SEO and structured data

- Do not index ad-only campaign pages or highly time-bound offers. Use `noindex` or keep them behind a non-indexed path.
- Index evergreen offers when search intent matches the promise. Add a clear title and meta description, internal links from relevant pages, and a plain question-and-answer FAQ only when it answers visitor questions.
- Add structured data only when it accurately describes visible page content and current search documentation supports that use case. Do not promise FAQ rich results or ranking gains; eligibility and display depend on each search engine.

## Common pitfalls

- Too many primary actions above the fold.
- Vague value propositions such as “streamline” or “optimize.”
- A long feature list without user outcomes.
- Verified proof separated from its claim.
- A mobile layout that harms readability.
- No clear next step.
